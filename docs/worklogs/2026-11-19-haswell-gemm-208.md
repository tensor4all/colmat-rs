# Haswell f64 GEMM: testing issue 208

## Decisions

- Do not adopt a smaller universal `kc` from this experiment. The simple claim
  that a 48 KiB A micropanel exceeding the 32 KiB L1 explains the slowdown is
  not established. `kc` also changes the blocking denominator
  `f = ceil(kc / 64)`, hence row/column chunks and branch selection. Holding
  that denominator at 8 reverses the 128-versus-512 result on 1024 cubed.
- The remaining microkernel gap is unresolved. The actual native f64 12x4
  kernel is generated assembly, not an intrinsics loop. Its normal load loop
  has 12 register accumulators, three A vector registers and one B broadcast
  register, no SIMD spills, no explicit prefetch and no k unrolling. This
  excludes compiler-generated SIMD spills in that loop as the explanation;
  lack of prefetch or unrolling is not itself evidence of a bottleneck.
- Increasing k with m/n held constant does not amortize packing relative to
  flops: both increase linearly in k. The issue's long-k measurement therefore
  does not isolate the remaining gap to the microkernel.

## Verification conclusions and constraints

Measured the registry `t4a-private-gemm-x86` 0.1.28 dependency directly through
its public GEMM API, with AVX256 f64 and one thread. This matches the native
path selected by colmat at `1b397a01ceeefd447430cb5cdea92e8e31ce7652`;
it is not a timing of the colmat wrapper or SVD. No upstream revision or
OpenBLAS/MKL was timed. No vendor source or binary was inspected.

On this E5-2699 v3, usual blocking at 1024 cubed gave median 35.50 GFLOPS
(kc128) versus 34.08 (kc512), about +4.2%. Fixed denominator8 gave 33.14 versus
33.92, about -2.3%. At 512x512x4096, usual blocking gave +6.3%, but ranges
of repetitions overlapped; fixed blocking gave essentially no change.
The 12x128x4096 reuse case gave 34.66 versus 37.62 GFLOPS: the larger panel
was faster, despite exceeding L1. This is inconsistent with a simple
capacity-only explanation, though packing and call/epilogue overhead still
confound the thin-shape comparison. Larger kc reduces the number of depth
panels and epilogues. No universal winning kc met the predeclared rule.

The complete 200-sample scan, protocol, source patch, generated-loop disassembly,
compiler/lock, and guard evidence are retained in
`colmat-benchmark/probes/haswell-208/README.md`.
Five shuffled rounds used CPU8, guarding its SMT sibling26; observer CPU0-3.
All per-process scalar-reference checks passed (full thin matrices, samples
for large matrices); the idle guard passed before/after on its first attempt.
This single-L3 host used the harness's physical-core reference mode, not full
cache-domain isolation; turbo stayed enabled. Some cells have substantial
spread. These are diagnostic results, not adoption or paired campaign evidence.
No production source or dependency was changed, so no PATCHSET experiment is added.

## What remains unverified

- A direct L1-miss measurement: perf is unavailable (`perf_event_paranoid=4`).
  A panel's size alone does not describe its reuse distance: in the square
  path, multiple A micropanels are traversed before reuse across B columns.
- Kernel-only throughput with explicitly packed operands and controlled cache
  residency, then separate packing / compute / epilogue timings for whole GEMM.
- Whether prefetch or loop scheduling improves those measured stages; neither
  has been tested by this scan. A remaining percentage relative to OpenBLAS
  was not remeasured here and must not be attributed to a specific stage.

## Follow-up: time spent in the 1024 cubed path

On the same Haswell at 1T and default kc512, the native dependency reached
33.90 GFLOP/s against 42.31 for system OpenBLAS 0.3.26 (Haswell dispatch via
public CBLAS), a 19.9% throughput deficit. Five shuffled repetitions had
disjoint ranges. This times the colmat backend directly, without the wrapper.

No standalone packing helpers were called for these operands. Sampling the
native calls with an unpacked A source attributed about 2.4% of instrumented
elapsed time to the entire calls that can pack A and about 96.5% to the other
microkernel calls. The 2.4% includes useful computation, so copying alone takes
less than that estimate. It does not measure downstream cache traffic caused
by the packing schedule. Instrumented median elapsed was 66.26 ms versus
63.35 ms for the clean native arm.

With prepacked inputs, reusing one 12x4 panel pair yielded 35.75 / 40.32 /
39.88 GFLOP/s at depths 128 / 256 / 512. Cycling through 16 pairs yielded
22.78 / 21.19 / 21.97. The larger working set has a strong effect and the
depth ordering depends on reuse. This does not isolate a particular cache
level: even one pair at depth 512 exceeds L1, and the 16-pair sets exceed L2.
Add and overwrite differed by less than 2% at every tested depth and reuse
setting. The tile test holds C hot, so it cannot rule out C traffic in full GEMM.

The observed time distribution points toward the microkernel's load/compute
schedule and data reuse as the next targets. A change to explicit packing
alone or to the hot-C epilogue is unlikely to close the observed gap.
No upstream revision or proprietary vendor was measured. Both idle guards
passed, all scalar checks passed, and turbo remained enabled. See the
`colmat-benchmark/probes/haswell-208-stages/protocol.md`.

## Follow-up: AVX2 load order and software prefetch

Two diagnostic generator changes were tested separately on the Haswell host:
move the first B broadcast before the three A loads, or prefetch A eight k
steps ahead with a remaining-depth check. Only the unmasked 12x4 body that
does not pack A was changed. The production dependency stays as shipped.

At 1024 cubed, the paired median throughput changes were −0.03% for early B
and +1.21% for prefetch. Both missed the predeclared 5% gate and their run
ranges overlapped the baseline. In the prepacked row-wise replay, prefetch
gave +2.22% at depth 512, with disjoint sample ranges, and −0.13% at depth
256. Early B gave −1.89% and −0.60%, respectively. The replay uses contiguous
packed B and tile-contiguous C. Its traversal approximates A reuse and does
not duplicate the full GEMM cache access pattern. The prefetch test measures
the instruction and its bounds check together.

All 45 scalar-reference spot checks passed and the physical-core idle guard
passed on its first attempt. Assembly inspection verified both instruction
changes. The measured dependency was private GEMM 0.1.28, matching the
colmat backend at 1b397a01. No upstream revision or OpenBLAS was timed in
this follow-up. `colmat-benchmark/probes/haswell-208-schedule/protocol.md`.

## B column spacing at fixed logical dimensions

At 1024 cubed the native throughput at B leading dimensions 1024, 1032 and
1040 was 33.49, 33.48 and 33.70 GFLOP/s. System OpenBLAS 0.3.26 Haswell
measured 41.75, 41.46 and 41.76. Relative to the original spacing, native
paired median changes were −0.19% and +0.20%. Each paired range crossed zero.
Offsetting successive B columns by one or two additional cache lines did
little to close the gap. This measures sensitivity to stride, not cache misses.

All 36 samples used the same allocated A/B/C base addresses in one process.
Logical B values were identical across strides and padding was filled with
NaNs. Sampled scalar reference products and the before/after core idle guard
passed. The native entry was registry 0.1.28 at default kc512, matching the
backend of colmat 1b397a01. No upstream revision was measured.
`colmat-benchmark/probes/haswell-208-ldb/protocol.md`.

## Depth unrolling after the stride test

Keeping the 12 original accumulators, two steps per loop branch gave +0.60%
and four gave +2.97% paired median throughput on 1024 cubed. For four steps,
all six whole-call samples exceeded the baseline range (34.35–35.19 versus
32.02–34.13 GFLOP/s), although the gain missed the 5% selection threshold.
A single prepacked hot tile at odd depth 511 gave +12.8% for overwrite and
+12.9% for add with four steps. The tile result includes the remainder loop.
The two builds alter loop size, alignment and branch frequency together, so
these timings do not isolate the reason for the benefit.

All 54 cases passed their scalar checks and the guard passed on its first
attempt. Same backend and host as the stride test. OpenBLAS and upstream were
not timed in this second scan. Retain four-way unrolling as a measured lead
on this CPU; the whole-call improvement is smaller than the isolated-tile
improvement. `colmat-benchmark/probes/haswell-208-unroll/protocol.md`.

## Targeting complete 1024 cubed GEMM against OpenBLAS

The stock 12x4 packing/order factorial gave 64.4 ms baseline, 61.7 ms
with only traversal changed, and 67.9–68.7 ms when B was packed, versus
51.5 ms for OpenBLAS. The four cases are relative to the same original
native traversal. The original square case already traverses rows inside
columns at the millikernel level. Changing its order is a comparison of
both loop choices, not evidence that the baseline lacks B reuse.

New assembly 8x6 and 12x4 kernels with a packed driver, optimized address
increments and 64-byte aligned scratch reduced the best classical scan
to 50.86 ms (OpenBLAS 49.33 ms). Longer k unrolls, alternate 16x3/24x2
shapes, and a permuted 12x4 accumulator layout did not establish a win.
Matrixmultiply 0.3.10 and t4a-gemm 0.20.1 also trailed the vendor at 1024.

Changing the algorithm reduced the arithmetic count. The original Strassen
seven-product identities (V. Strassen, Numer. Math. 13, 1969) were implemented
independently with custom assembly leaves. Recursive quadrant storage and
explicit conversions/additions used too much of the time saved in the leaves.
Fusing input sums into panel packing and combining seven outputs in a single
pass gave a faster one-level candidate. It changes rounding and intermediate
range and requires reusable workspace. No vendor implementation was consulted.

An independent balanced comparison locked the one-level 8x6 candidate at
kc176, mc96 and compared on the same allocations to system 0.3.26 and Julia
1.13.3's 0.3.30, both Haswell 1T. At 1024 its median was 48.57 ms vs 48.59
and 48.85 ms. It won 7/12 and 10/12 pairs respectively, missing the
predeclared 11/12 target. At 511 and 513 both vendors were faster in all
six rounds. All outputs passed comparison to a classical reference on dense
pseudorandom inputs. The Julia library was isolated in its own ELF namespace
after linking both vendors into a shared namespace crashed on the Julia call.
That failed recording was excluded in its entirety. `colmat-benchmark/probes/haswell-208-paired/protocol.md`.

## Winning candidate at 1024, slower at 511 and 513

A one-level Strassen prototype packs signed quadrant sums, transposes the B
panels in four-row vectors, and uses a custom 8x6 AVX2/FMA kernel with four
depth steps per loop branch. It stores each of the seven intermediate products
as contiguous 8x6 tiles. The final quadrant combination reads those tiles
and writes ordinary column-major C. This layout removes the strided
intermediate C updates and scalar edge-tile copies from the compute loop.
The independent comparison used kc176, mc96 and strips of eight for A packing.

The table contains median milliseconds per full call, including all packing,
addition, fringe handling and recombination. Buffers were allocated before
timing. Same buffer addresses, one thread, CPU8, shared-L3 reference guard,
turbo enabled, Haswell Xeon E5-2699 v3. The driver used the public OpenBLAS
APIs and verified one thread in each library. No vendor code was inspected.

| n | Prototype | OpenBLAS 0.3.26 | Julia OpenBLAS 0.3.30 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1024 | 46.880 | 48.781 | 48.841 |
| 511 | 7.170 | 6.557 | 6.530 |
| 513 | 7.125 | 6.692 | 6.752 |

At 1024 the paired median speedups were 1.0405x and 1.0414x respectively.
All 12 paired rounds favored the frozen candidate against both versions
(predeclared minimum 11). The candidate lost all six paired rounds against
each vendor at both smaller sizes. Every output was compared against the
classical system OpenBLAS reference on the same dense pseudorandom matrices.
Additional scalar dot products covered sampled rows and columns including
the last. Maximum absolute difference at 1024 was 2.0e-14. The physical-core
guard passed before and after on its first attempt.

The result concerns an explicitly experimental algorithm with a different
rounding and intermediate overflow profile, at a fixed host, build, layout
and thread count. It is not an adoption result for the colmat dispatcher.
No upstream faer revision was measured. The holdout followed explorations of
packing, register tile sizes, intermediate layout and loop bounds. The
positive finding applies to the whole candidate; the holdout does not
attribute every millisecond of the difference to one change.

## Public matmul at the three reported sizes

The earlier prototype copied both odd inputs and copied the cropped output.
The new one-level product at 511 treats the missing row and column of a
512-square problem as zeros while packing. At 513 it computes the 512-square
core, adds the last outer product during recombination, and computes the last
row and column explicitly. An exploratory 256-deep, 64-row block with
vectorized recombination took 6.24–6.26 ms at 511 and 6.32–6.41 ms at 513
(two batches each). The scan selected a candidate; the independent public
API run below decides the comparison.

The opt-in Cargo feature `experimental-strassen` routes eligible calls to
`colmat::linalg::matmul::matmul` through this implementation. The route
requires f64, an initialized destination, unit scale, overwrite semantics,
sequential execution, column-major operands and AVX2/FMA. It accepts separate
leading dimensions, including padded columns. The 1024-square path uses
176-deep and 96-row blocks. Each thread acquires its own reusable buffer on
first use or growth. The comparisons time steady-state use. The public path
includes dispatch, packing, the seven products and recombination. Strassen's
intermediate quadrant sums can amplify cancellation or overflow on finite
inputs. This feature is experimental and off by default.

The first public validation used one batch of five calls per provider in
each of twelve fresh processes per size. The median times for colmat, system
OpenBLAS and Julia OpenBLAS respectively were 6.396/6.661/6.774 ms at 511,
6.570/6.870/6.889 at 513 and 47.853/49.765/50.423 at 1024. Paired wins
were 10/11, 9/10 and 10/11 out of twelve against system/Julia respectively.
This missed the all-cells 11/12 criterion despite a lower median in all six
comparisons. The two odd-size medians improved over the previous pad-and-copy
prototype. All full-output checks passed. The library suite passed (843
passed, 162 ignored), including distinct padded leading dimensions and NaN
padding at all three sizes. A global counting allocator observed zero heap
allocations on the second public call at each size.

A second recursion level with materialized outer sums was slower (7.35–7.55
ms at 511). Keeping small blocks of all seven products local gave
6.42–6.54 ms at 511, with no clear gain over the existing schedule. For the
one-level algorithm a two-sample randomized scan compared packed-A prefetch
distances 0/192/384/768/1536 bytes and panel gaps of 0/8/24 doubles. At
1536/8 the medians were 6.12, 6.32 and 46.44 ms, versus 6.22, 6.34 and
46.95 at 0/0. We froze the same distance and gap for all three sizes. The
scan cannot establish the marginal benefit of either constant in isolation.

### Independent results for the final feature

The public binary was built with colmat's default features plus
`experimental-strassen`, Rust 1.96.0, release, one codegen unit and
`-C target-cpu=haswell`. Against system OpenBLAS 0.3.26 and Julia 1.13.3's
0.3.30, the 12-round series gave 12/11 wins at 511, 8/9 at 513, and 11/11 at
1024 respectively (out of 12 per vendor). Thus 513 missed the predeclared
11/12 target. The corresponding paired median speedups were 1.053/1.070,
1.055/1.053 and 1.063/1.070.

Before either series ran, a second series of 24 independent rounds was
specified for the same binary. Each round used a fresh process, new dense
pseudorandom inputs, shared A/B/C addresses, 250 ms warmup per provider and
a five-call batch. All six orders occurred four times per size. Both vendors
were set and checked at one thread. All full-output and scalar spot checks
passed. The maximum absolute error was 2.82e-14 in this series.

| n | colmat ms | System ms | Julia ms | wins/24 system, Julia | paired median speedup system, Julia | simultaneous lower bounds |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 511 | 6.252 | 6.574 | 6.613 | 24, 24 | 1.049x, 1.054x | 1.030x, 1.039x |
| 513 | 6.395 | 6.768 | 6.819 | 19, 23 | 1.055x, 1.062x | 1.014x, 1.014x |
| 1024 | 45.928 | 49.226 | 49.364 | 24, 24 | 1.067x, 1.070x | 1.056x, 1.059x |

The lower bound is the sixth smallest paired ratio of 24, a conservative
one-sided simultaneous 95% median bound across the six comparisons under
independent rounds. All six exceeded 1, and all estimated median ratios
exceeded the specified 1.02. The original and the confirmation results are
reported separately. These are steady-state Haswell shared-cache references.
Eight attempts to obtain a full shared-L3 idle window failed before
confirmation timing began. CPU3 carried roughly 9–10% user load. The
physical-core check passed before and after on CPU8 and its SMT sibling in
both series. Turbo was enabled. A full acceptance campaign, cold-start
timings, other architectures, and end-to-end SVD remain unverified.

The final feature-enabled library suite passed with 843 successes, 0 failures
and 162 ignored tests. Results, sources, patch, both protocols and the
allocator check are in the local archive
`colmat-benchmark/probes/haswell-208-public/haswell-208-public-reproducer.tar.gz`.
