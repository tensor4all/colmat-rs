# LU owning route at 1024/1536: the remaining price is the factor buffer's first touch, not the factorization

Issue: [tensor4all/colmat-rs#191](https://github.com/tensor4all/colmat-rs/issues/191), follow-up to
#210 ("close the 1024 owning gap").

Measured revision: `a5de9b36` (`origin/main`, the merge of #210) for the fork arm, `faer-v0.24.4`
(`0539947f`) for the upstream control row, both in one `fbench` binary
(`tensor4all/colmat-benchmark` `6c4b29b`), MKL **2026.2** (`/opt/intel/oneapi/mkl/2026.2`,
`libmkl_rt.so.3`, Intel OpenMP `iomp5`). One host, one session: **EVO-X1Pro**, AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 470
(Zen 5), 24 logical CPUs, two L3 domains (CPUs `0-3,12-15` = 16 MiB and `4-11,16-23`), THP =
`madvise`, `AnonHugePages` = 0. Measured CPUs: 1T `4`, 4T `4-7`, 8T `4-11` (one L3 domain); the
observer shell runs pinned to `0-3,12-15`. Nothing here is comparable with the evo2 records.

**Purpose of the run**: diagnosis and a decision to ship or not, so none of these numbers is a
publication run and none of them may enter the published site (`R-performance-95`).

**Yardstick**: MKL, because the issue is defined against it; the *finding* does not depend on it — it
is the owning route against the same library's in-place route, and the in-place route is the one that
lines up with MKL's row shape (see §5).

## 1. The measured gap, with the harness row that actually calls the API

The harness's fork `lu`/`solve` owning rows time the composite
`explicit_ops::partial_lu_of` (`fbench/src/backend.rs:1906`): `a.to_owned()`, then `lu_in_place`, then
`split_lu` into two fresh matrices — three allocations, and never the `PartialPivLu` the public API
returns. Grid B below is the same binary built from a locally patched harness whose fork rows call
`PartialPivLu::new` and check through `DenseSolveCore::reconstruct`; the patch is local only, its
sha256 and diff are in §9, and every row of its
`verify` run passes (`320/320 ok`, "all comparisons within tolerance", n = 32..1024, f64/c64, C/F, 8T).

`fbench run --threads 8 --n 768,1024,1536 --dtype f64,c64 --family lu --rows
faer-fork,faer-upstream,lapack-mkl --reps 5 --prime-ms 250 --rotate-rows 1,2 --statistic median`,
median of the two rotated sets, ms per call, order F:

| n | dtype | colmat `in_place` | colmat owning | MKL | `in_place`/MKL | owning/MKL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1024 | c64 | 18.29 | 24.12 | 21.22 | **0.86** | **1.14** |
| 1024 | f64 | 5.80 | 6.44 | 5.26 | 1.10 | 1.22 |
| 1536 | c64 | 53.58 | 65.55 | 71.50 | **0.75** | **0.92** |
| 1536 | f64 | 16.36 | 22.09 | 16.60 | 0.99 | 1.33 |

Order C is the same shape (`1024 c64`: owning 25.01 / `in_place` 21.20 / MKL 21.87 → 1.14);
`1536 c64 C` 67.24 / 65.11 / 77.42 → 0.87.

Two things follow, and they are the answer to the issue's premise:

1. **At 1024 c64 the kernel is ahead of MKL and the owning route is 1.14, not 1.31.** The issue's
   "c64 1.31x vs MKL" and "f64 1.06x vs MKL" are not vs-MKL ratios: they are the *owning/in-place*
   ratios of the #210 record (`REPORT.md` §6: "still 1.23x in-place (c64 4T) and 1.31x (c64 8T)"),
   quoted with the wrong yardstick. The #210 report also says the campaign has no 1024 cell, so no
   vs-MKL figure existed for 1024 when the issue was written.
2. Where the owning route *is* behind (1024 f64 1.22, 1536 f64 1.33) the kernel is at or near MKL and
   the owning route adds 0.64 / 5.73 ms on top of it. That add-on is what this record is about.

The straddling sizes are `1024 c64` (32 MiB factor buffer) and `1536 f64` (37.7 MB); `768 c64`
(18.9 MB) and `1024 f64` (16 MiB) add only 0.31 / 0.36 ms at 8T. The size, not the dtype, picks the
behaviour.

## 2. Where the owning route's cost is

One process per cell, `lu191probe` in `~/work/colmat-191lu/probe`, each route reporting the
`/proc/self/stat` field-10 minor-fault delta per call. `in_place` allocates the same permutation
vectors and the same `MemBuffer` inside the clock that `new_imp` allocates, so the difference is not
an artefact of hoisted allocations.

| n | dtype | order | T | `in_place` | owning | overhead | faults/call (owning) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 768 | c64 | F | 8 | 8.94 | 9.60 | +0.66 | 46 |
| 1024 | c64 | F | 8 | 19.16 | 25.16 | +5.99 | **8193** |
| 1024 | c64 | F | 4 | 31.10 | 38.43 | +7.33 | **8193** |
| 1024 | c64 | F | 1 | 102.36 | 117.93 | +15.58 | **8193** |
| 1024 | f64 | F | 8 | 6.22 | 6.58 | +0.36 | 68 |
| 1536 | c64 | F | 8 | 55.54 | 66.48 | +10.94 | **18436** |
| 1536 | f64 | F | 8 | 17.37 | 23.55 | +6.18 | **9217** |
| 1536 | f64 | C | 8 | 20.70 | 24.28 | +3.58 | **9217** |

Every cell with fewer than 100 faults per call has an overhead below 0.8 ms at 4T/8T; every cell with
8193 faults or more has 3.6-15.6 ms. The overhead is the fault count, not the shape.

**Stage decomposition** (throwaway diagnostic build, `LU191_STAGE` read once in `new_imp`; the branch
itself is untouched), 1024 c64 F, ms/call, 20 calls:

| stage | 8T | faults/call | 1T | faults/call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| allocate the factor buffer only | 0.93 | 1 | 0.90 | 1 |
| + copy the input in | 3.54 | 4097 | 8.10 | 4097 |
| + factor in place | 22.10 | 4097 | 110.49 | 4097 |
| + split into dense `L`/`U` (= today) | 24.74 | 8193 | 118.11 | 8193 |

So at 8T: allocation 0.93 ms (mmap/unmap of 32 MiB, no touch), copy 2.61 ms (its 4096 faults are all
of the packed block), factor 18.56 ms (no new pages — it works inside the block the copy faulted),
split 2.65 ms (its 4096 faults are all of the square block, which nothing has touched yet). The
factorization is 75 % of the call and is not the difference; the copy and the split are, and each of
them is dominated by first-touching a 16 MiB block rather than by moving bytes.

At 1024 f64 (16 MiB, no faults) the same decomposition is allocate 0.36, copy 0.32, factor 5.71,
split 0.38: the owning route's whole overhead is 0.55 ms of traffic.

## 3. Why the buffer's first touch costs what it costs

`new_imp` allocates one block of `ld_p * n + ld_s * size` elements: 32 MiB at 1024 c64 (exactly
33 554 432 B) and 37.7 MB at 1536 f64. glibc serves a request that large with `mmap` every time —
`DEFAULT_MMAP_THRESHOLD_MAX` is 33 554 432 B, and the chunk needs 16 B more than that — so every call
maps fresh pages, faults them one at a time while the copy and the split write them, and unmaps them
at the end. Measured in isolation (`lu191alloc`, allocate, write every page, free):

| bytes | MiB | ms/call | faults/call |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 MiB | 8 | 0.29 | 409.7 |
| 16 MiB | 16 | 0.70 | 614.5 |
| **32 MiB** | 32 | **12.70** | **8193** |
| 33 MiB | 33 | 12.89 | 8449 |
| 64 MiB | 64 | 25.50 | 16385 |

and the diagnostic checks that the cliff is the total size and not the block count or the library's
choices:

- **two live 16 MiB blocks (32 MiB total)**: 12.49 ms, 8163 faults/call — the same. Splitting the
  buffer would not help; the one-allocation representation of #210 is not the cause.
- **`MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_=113246208`** (108 MiB): 12.64 ms, 8160 faults/call — no change, and the
  real route does not move either (24.75 → 24.44 ms).
- **`MALLOC_TOP_PAD_=67108864`**: the isolated loop drops to 1.41 ms (4096 faults on the *first*
  iteration only), but the real route stays at 24.48 ms / 8193 faults/call. The environment knob does
  not transfer to a process that allocates anything else between the calls.
- **`MADV_HUGEPAGE` on the same bytes** (`lu191thp`, diagnostic only): 32 MiB 12.52 ms / 8193
  faults without, 12.48 ms / 8193 with; 64 MiB 25.79 / 16385 either way. This host's THP policy is
  `madvise`, and the hint still does nothing because a `malloc` block is not 2 MiB-aligned, so no huge
  page can cover it. The search for an allocator-level remedy is closed on measurement.

**The prize, measured.** The owning route with L/U written into buffers that are already resident
(`owning_reuse`: `lu_in_place` plus the same split into a preallocated pair) is 20.38 ms at 1024 c64
F 8T against 24.76 for today's route, and 18.74 against 23.53 at 1536 f64 F; at 1024 f64 it is 6.88
against 6.67, i.e. *slower*, because its split is serial and there are no faults to save. Removing
first-touch provisioning entirely is worth **≈4.4-4.8 ms at 8T**, which is 80-90 % of the owning
route's overhead at both straddling sizes (5.83-5.99 ms): at 1024 c64 the owning route would land at
≈20.4 ms, i.e. 0.96× MKL.

Both scans on `lu_in_place` alone (the `in_place` cell of table 1 is what they can move), 20 calls,
one process per cell, diagnostic build, ms/call:

| `recursion_threshold` (leaf/panel width) | 1024 c64 8T | 1024 c64 1T | 1536 f64 8T | 768 c64 8T | 1024 f64 8T |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 19.19 | 102.95 | 17.14 | 9.04 | 6.16 |
| **8 (today)** | 19.02 | 102.95 | 17.44 | 8.89 | 6.26 |
| 16 | 19.53 | 103.14 | 18.96 | 9.31 | 6.75 |
| 32 | 21.55 | 104.43 | 20.52 | 9.92 | 8.16 |

| level block size (trailing-update width `b`) | 1024 c64 8T | 1024 c64 1T | 1536 f64 8T | 768 c64 8T |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **512 (today)** | 18.98 | 102.66 | 17.40 | 8.88 |
| 256 | 18.71 | 104.19 | 17.12 | 8.97 |

The leaf is already at its optimum (16 and 32 are 2.7-13 % worse); halving the level block is a 1.4 %
win at 8T and a 1.5 % loss at 1T, i.e. inside this host's 3 % between-set disagreement
(`R-performance-04`). **No width change is a candidate**, and the 1024 f64 `in_place`/MKL 1.10 is the
kernel's own standing at that size, not a blocking choice.

## 5. Why the MKL row is not the owning row

The harness times the vendor row as `getrf` on a prepared, reused buffer, and does the L/U split only
in its untimed check (`fbench/src/lapack.rs:1093`), while both faer rows' owning op allocates and
materialises inside the clock. `docs/reliable-measurement.md:107` says the owning faer route is "the
call a user makes … not the comparison with the vendor row". The like-for-like cell is therefore
`in_place`: 0.86× MKL at 1024 c64, 0.75× at 1536 c64, 1.10× at 1024 f64, 0.99× at 1536 f64.

The fork rows' *owning* op also diverged from the API it claims to time until this record (§1).
That is a benchmark-repo defect, not a library one: `R-performance-06` asks for a case that detects a
path divergence like this. Reported, not fixed here — different repository, and this change is
docs-only.

## 6. What would remove the first-touch term, and why none of it ships here

| option | what it buys | why it is not this PR |
|---|---|---|
| the caller's own buffer — `lu_in_place` | all of it (that is the 0.86× cell) | already public; the owning route exists for callers who want `L()`/`U()` |
| a factor buffer that outlives the call (pool, per-thread working memory, or an owned reusable `PartialPivLu`) | ≈4.4-4.8 ms at 8T (§3) | ownership and lifetime architecture: who owns the pool, what a dropped factorization returns to, what happens across teams. Parked for a design decision, not a tweak |
| allocate the factor buffer from a 2 MiB-aligned or prefaulted mapping | ≈4.4-4.8 ms at 8T, if the mapping is aligned | a second allocation path inside a numerical library, platform-specific, breaks `no_std`; `MADV_HUGEPAGE` measured as a non-lever anyway (§3) |
| fuse the split into the panel loop (C1 in `IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md`) | ≤0.2 ms: the split's 2.65 ms at 8T is 4096 faults plus 40 MiB, and the cold re-read it would remove is 16 MiB of that | below this host's noise, and it threads a destination through the recursion for it |
| write the square block's structural zeros earlier (C2) | ≤0.8 ms (the untouched page tails of the square block, and only with a zeroed allocation) | needs a size-conditioned allocation path, and would *add* a full memset at the sizes glibc serves from the arena (`R-performance-07`) |
| non-temporal stores (C4) | nothing under a fault-dominated term (§2) | measured precondition fails |

## 7. Decisions

- **No library change in this PR.** The owning route's residual at 1024 c64 / 1536 f64 is first-touch
  page provisioning of a buffer that the API requires and the allocator will not reuse. The measured
  levers that remain are API/lifetime decisions, not kernel changes, and every kernel-side candidate
  is under the noise floor with its own complexity cost (`R-performance-01`, `R-performance-08`).
- **The issue's target is met where the kernel decides it and priced where the API decides it**:
  1024 c64 owning/MKL is 1.14 (target ≤1.20) and the in-place cell is 0.86; 1024 f64 owning/MKL is
  1.22 with the kernel at 1.10, so what remains there is a kernel standing, not an owning-route defect.
- **The next lever is a design decision, not a patch**: a factor buffer whose pages survive between
  calls. Owner: shinaoka, as an API/lifetime decision.
- **Benchmark-repo follow-up**: the fork owning rows should time `PartialPivLu` (patch in §9), and a
  case that would catch the divergence is `R-performance-06`'s ask.

## 8. Not checked

- `solve` was measured only through `fbench` (grid B, verified and timed per §1); the probe routes are
  `lu` only. `PartialPivLu`'s solve shares the same factor buffer, so its owning overhead is the same
  term, but that is an argument, not a measurement.
- Orders C/F were measured for every size in the probe grid. `fbench` grid B was run at 8T only, two
  rotated sets; its 1T/4T cells do not exist, so every 1T/4T number here is from the probe (which
  covers both orders and all three thread counts). Grid A has 1T/4T/8T at one rotated set and 8T at
  three.
- No 2048 cell: the task bounds this to 768/1024/1536, and at 2048 the factor buffer is 128 MiB.
- The host has no `x86-v4`/`nightly` configuration and boost is on; nothing here is comparable with
  the evo2 records.
